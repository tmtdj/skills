# Scoring

The scale is adapted from Zapier's AI fluency rubric (Unacceptable, Capable, Adoptive, Transformative). Zapier uses it in interviews. A CV is only words on a page, so this skill uses a written rule. Two people scoring the same CV should get the same result.

## The rule

| Level | Points | What the page must show |
|---|---|---|
| Unacceptable | 0 | No line shows the skill. |
| Capable | 1 | The CV claims it, or names a tool, course or AI product, with no outcome. |
| Adoptive | 2 | An outcome with a number and a baseline (from X to Y, or X compared with the old way), or a repeatable system: a workflow the person built that produces a named output and runs regularly or is used by others. |
| Transformative | 3 | The person changed how a team or company works, with a before and after, or proof a stranger could check (public link, named test set, live users, named reviewer). A team size alone isn't enough. |

How to apply it:
- Score what the page says. Don't give credit for what you guess they did.
- Take the highest level any single line earns. One line can count for more than one skill.
- If you are unsure between two levels, take the lower one.
- A team result scores for the person's own part. If the line doesn't say what they did, it's Capable at most.
- A number without a baseline is still only Capable for an AI skill.
- Skill 1 (outcomes from AI) needs a result that came from AI work. A metric moved with a before and after, where the line says AI or agents did part of the work, is Adoptive. Add a team or company-wide change, or proof a stranger could check, and it's Transformative. "Improved conversion with AI" with no number is Capable. An AI product or AI work named with no outcome claimed at all is Unacceptable for skill 1 (it may still score for building or speed). Cycle time, release cadence and output speed score for skill 3, not skill 1, unless the line also names a user or business metric they moved. A strong outcome line also scores for the skill it shows (speed, building, delegating and so on).
- Skill 2 (judgement) counts any product or business decision with a result, with or without AI. Every other skill needs AI in the line.
- Using a chat or coding tool yourself, however often ("use ChatGPT daily"), is Capable for skill 3 (speeding up work). It becomes Adoptive only with an outcome and baseline, or when it's a workflow they built that produces something named (releases, reports, prototypes) on a schedule or for others.
- A skill named only in the skills list ("prompt engineering") is Capable for that skill.
- Skill 4 (delegating) and skill 7 (when it is an agent team the person runs): running agents across several named business functions (for example code, marketing, copy, web, video, audio, ads) for a live product the person runs is Adoptive, with no output counts needed. Live product links, or other proof a stranger could check, make it Transformative. A before and after can still raise a line if they gave one. Never ask for output counts such as ads a week, videos a month or releases a month. Ask only for the outcome, cost, a before and after, how the work is checked, and proof links.
- Skill 7 (getting a team onto AI) counts advisory clients and agent teams the person runs, when named. "Run my apps alone with a team of AI agents", with no functions named, is Capable for skill 7.
- Skill 10 (learning speed) needs evidence of change over time (tried, dropped, changed) or a course. Naming the tools someone uses doesn't count for skill 10.
- The person's answers to "what do you do with AI that isn't on here?" go in a second column, scored with the same rule on the facts they gave. Never assume a number they didn't give. Never mix it into the on-page score.
- Pre-agent AI work (for example a GPT-3.5 or GPT-4 chat feature, a machine learning feature, whatever the date) counts for skill 1 (outcomes) if it moved a metric, skill 2 (judgement), skill 6 (building), and skill 5 (evals) if they measured the quality of the AI output. It doesn't count for skill 4 (delegating to agents).
- Pre-AI work (A/B testing, budgets, team leadership) doesn't score on the AI skills. It goes in the bridges list.

## The ten skills

In priority order, the same as the carousel this skill comes from.

1. **Outcomes from AI.** A result that came from AI work: a metric moved, with a before and after. Shows as: conversion, revenue, retention, activation, support load or another named user or business metric, from X to Y, where the line says what AI or agents did. Release speed alone is skill 3. Every other skill line should also carry the outcome it moved.
2. **Judgement.** Picking the right problem when doing the work is cheap. Shows as: decisions made and what happened, ideas killed fast, an AI product chosen and launched with a result.
3. **Speeding up work with AI.** AI used in core work, with a before and after. Shows as: cycle time or time to ship compared with the old way.
4. **Delegating to agents.** Choosing what to hand an agent, writing a clear brief and a definition of done, reviewing what comes back. Shows as: the named functions they run with agents, how they check the work, and any proof link or before and after. Several named functions on a live product is enough for Adoptive. Do not ask for output counts.
5. **Evals (checking AI output).** Saying what good looks like for AI output and checking real outputs against it. Shows as: a test set of N cases, a pass rate from A% to B%, a quality checklist AI work is judged against.
6. **Building it yourself.** Making a working version yourself with AI tools. Shows as: shipped apps, prototypes, demos, repos, ideas tested with users.
7. **Getting a team onto AI.** Getting a whole team working the new way. Shows as: a team moved to agent workflows, with a before and after if they have one. Counts an employer team, an advisory client's team, or a team of AI agents the person runs, when the line names it. For an agent team the person runs, several named functions on a live product is enough for Adoptive; live product links make it Transformative. Never as heads removed.
8. **Cost per task.** Knowing what an AI task costs and bringing it down. Shows as: cost per run from X to Y. Evidence that employers ask for this is thin, so weight it low unless the role is technical.
9. **Context engineering.** Giving AI the information it needs to do the job well: docs, examples, rules and data, set up before it starts. Shows as: context files, prompt or skill libraries a team uses.
10. **Learning speed (the "slope").** How fast their AI use is improving. Zapier calls this the slope. Shows as: what they tried, dropped and changed in the last 12 months.

## Weights by target role

3 = core, 2 = useful, 1 = minor. Outcomes from AI is 3 for every role. If the target role has direct reports, set skill 7 (getting a team onto AI) to 3. If they name two target roles, use the first. If they give none, use their latest job title.

| Skill | PM | Engineer | Designer | Data / analyst | Marketer | Ops | Founder / exec |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Outcomes from AI | 3 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 3 |
| 2 Judgement | 3 | 2 | 3 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 3 |
| 3 Speeding up work | 3 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 3 |
| 4 Delegating to agents | 3 | 3 | 2 | 2 | 3 | 3 | 2 |
| 5 Evals | 3 | 3 | 2 | 3 | 2 | 2 | 1 |
| 6 Building it yourself | 3 | 2 | 3 | 2 | 1 | 1 | 3 |
| 7 Getting a team onto AI | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
| 8 Cost per task | 1 | 2 | 1 | 2 | 1 | 2 | 2 |
| 9 Context engineering | 2 | 3 | 1 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 1 |
| 10 Learning speed | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 |

Founder / exec covers founder, CPO, CTO, VP and Head of roles. For a role not listed, pick the closest column and say which.

## Overall level

1. Work out the weighted average: sum of (weight x points) divided by sum of weights.
2. Map it: below 0.5 = Unacceptable. 0.5 to 1.49 = Capable. 1.5 to 2.49 = Adoptive. 2.5 or more = Transformative.
3. Show the sum and the score to two decimals for the page and for "with your answers", so the person can check it and see it move. Levels are coarse, so the number is what shows progress.
4. Impact for ranking recommendations, in two groups. Already true, not on the page: weight x (answers points minus page points). Needs new work: weight x (3 minus answers points). The first group is ranked first, because it needs no new work.

## Bridges from pre-AI strengths

| They already have | The AI version to show, if true |
|---|---|
| A/B testing, experiments | Evals: a test set for AI output, and its pass rate |
| Budget or P&L ownership | Cost per task: what an AI workflow costs per run, and how they brought it down |
| Leading teams, hiring, coaching | Getting a team onto AI, and delegating to agents |
| Writing specs, briefs, PRDs | Context engineering: the briefs and context files agents work from |
| Research, user testing | Faster learning loops: ideas tested with AI prototypes, compared with the old way |
| Process or ops design | Agent workflows with a checkpoint and a named reviewer |
| Shipping products without code (with engineers) | Building it yourself: shipping working versions with AI coding tools |
| Results with a before and after | Outcomes from AI: the same kind of result, where AI did part of the work |

Write each bridge as one line. Only suggest the AI version if it's true. Never write it into the CV for them.
